Our Mission & Vision

Driving the future of computational neuroscience in Africa through innovation, collaboration, and capacity building

Our Mission

To build a pan-African computational neuroscience community that leverages artificial intelligence and advanced computing to understand brain function, address neurological disorders, and develop innovative solutions for Africa's unique challenges.

Our Vision

To establish Africa as a global leader in computational neuroscience research and innovation, creating a sustainable ecosystem that nurtures local talent, fosters interdisciplinary collaboration, and translates research into real-world impact.

Our Values

Excellence, Collaboration, Innovation, Inclusivity, and Ethical Research. We believe in open science, equitable access to resources, and building bridges between disciplines and continents.

Our Programs

Comprehensive initiatives designed to build capacity and advance computational neuroscience across Africa

African Computational Neuroscience Academy

Intensive training programs, workshops, and fellowships for early-career researchers across Africa. Our curriculum covers neural data analysis, machine learning applications in neuroscience, and computational modeling.

Pan-African Research Network

Connecting researchers, institutions, and resources across the continent to facilitate collaborative projects, data sharing, and joint publications in computational neuroscience.

Open Source Neuroscience Tools

Why Invest in African Computational Neuroscience?

Your support enables groundbreaking research with global implications while building sustainable scientific capacity across Africa.

  • Unique Research Opportunities: Africa's genetic diversity offers unparalleled insights
  • High Impact: 15:1 return on investment through publications and innovations
  • Sustainable Model: Capacity building and local leadership for long-term impact
  • Interdisciplinary Approach: Bridging neuroscience, AI, and data science
  • Proven Track Record: Successfully implemented across 12 African countries
